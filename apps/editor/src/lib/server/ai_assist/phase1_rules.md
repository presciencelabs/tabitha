# Phase 1 Encoding Rules (TBTA / TaBiThA)

This is the full itemized ruleset for writing valid Phase 1 encoding.

Notes throughout distinguish the **full notation** (used for most Phase 1
work — includes brackets, explicit pronoun referents, underscore tense/
implicit markers) from **He1** (a lighter-weight shorthand variant — no
brackets, `<<...>>` / `<...>` for implicit info, more natural-sounding
wording in a few specific spots). Where a rule doesn't mention He1
separately, apply it the same way in both.

0.1. Do not use third person pronouns except for "each-other". First and
second person pronouns (like "I" and "you") and "each-other" must have the
referent indicated in parentheses, like "I(John) talked to Mary". Using the
new method for He1, write the first instance of a pronoun in a verse the old
way, but after that you can leave the pronouns.

0.2. Only use level 2 words (magenta in the ontology) or level 3 words (dark
green) in phase 1 text in pairings ("son/descendant"), explications ("hard
hat" instead of "helmet"), or complex alternates (preceded by "(complex)",
normally paired with a "(simple)" version). If you are unsure of a word's complexity level, prefer a
simpler everyday word, or pair the simple word with the complex one. Level 2
and level 3 words are currently treated the same functionally.

0.3. Write hyphenated ontology words hyphenated ("John take-away Mary's
food"). Default verb tense is "discourse"; for a different tense use
underline notation ("John take-away _future Mary's food").

0.3 (second). Each clause has exactly one verb. Subordinate (not
independent) clauses are enclosed in square brackets. Total left brackets =
total right brackets in a sentence.

0.4. No more than four levels of embedded brackets — simplify or split the
sentence if you'd exceed that. Keep He1 sentences from getting too
complicated even without visible brackets.

0.5. Place event subordinate clauses and adverbial prepositional phrases so
the sentence has the intended scope. "We(people) know [Jesus died because of
our(people's) `_incl` bad-B actions]" — NOT "We(people) know [Jesus died]
because of our(people's) `_incl` bad-B actions" (wrong scope).

0.6. Put a prepositional phrase in a relative clause if it modifies a noun:
"John talked to the man [who previously was in Bethlehem]", not "John talked
to the man previously in Bethlehem". Applies to He1 too (without brackets).

0.7. Start each relative clause with a relativizer ("that"/"who"/"whom"):
"the man [WHO was in Bethlehem]". Do NOT put "that" at the start of an
object-complement (patient) clause: "John knew [Mary was in Bethlehem]", not
"John knew [that Mary was in Bethlehem]". For He1, word object-complement
clauses naturally.

0.8. Never use "when"/"where" as a relativizer. "The time [that John saw
Mary at] was late", not "The time [when John saw Mary] was late". Preferred
in He1 too, to avoid confusion with adverbial clauses.

0.9. Avoid repeated nouns in object-complement clauses if unnatural; put the
repeatable noun in the patient clause: "John helped [Mary to find food]",
not "John helped Mary [Mary to find food]" or "John helped Mary [to find
food]". For He1, word it naturally.

0.10. If a noun was mentioned previously, normally use "that" instead of
"the" when referring to it again.

0.11. Mark implicit information: parentheses-based notation for clauses,
underscore notation for the phrase containing the word before the
underscore. Don't make a head implicit if its modifiers aren't also
implicit — not "the person _implicit [who was in that house]" if only
"person" is implicit. For He1: `<<...>>` = regular implicit info, `<...>` =
necessary implicit info.

0.12. Precede a quotation with the speaker and a verb like "say"/"ask". If
both the subject and the verb are implicit: "X _implicitNecessary said
_implicitNecessary, [\"...\"" (or `<...>` in He1). It's fine to open with a
quotation if preceding wording already signaled one was coming, e.g. "X said
the following words. _verseBoundary "quotation"" or "X spoke to Y.
_verseBoundary "quotation"".

0.13. For a passive, include the agent with "by": "John was hit by a
soldier", or "John was hit by a soldier _implicitActiveAgent" ("<<by a
soldier>>" in He1) if the agent is implicit.

0.14. Rhetorical questions are preceded by "(rhetorical)", "(yesrhetorical)"
or "(norhetorical)", followed by a statement version preceded by
"(statement)".

0.15. Use perfect tense ("John has gone") only where simple past + "recently"
or "previously" would also work ("John recently went").

0.16. Don't use "even", "any", or "own" (as in "John's own house").

0.17. Use "all of" rather than bare "all" unless the noun is generic: "all of
those people" but "God loves all people" (generic).

0.18. Imperatives (commands) must show the agent and use "(imp)": "You(John)
(imp) go to the town" → generates as "Go to the town". For He1, you may write
it the way the English is generated.

0.19. Don't treat "begin"/"start", "stop", "finish", "continue to" + another
verb as a separate verb — they trigger features on the following verb, not a
clause: "John started talking to Mary", NOT "John started [talking to
Mary]".

0.20. Modifiers meant to apply to multiple elements of a coordinate noun
phrase must either be repeated for each noun phrase or use "_coordinate"
notation.

0.21. No isolated noun phrases with no valid sentence function. Not "The next
day [rest]" — needs a relation: "On the next day, ...". Nouns must be a verb
argument or used with a relation.

0.22. "and" before what looks like a subordinate clause is only valid if
it's the final clause of the same type as a preceding subordinate clause:
"The man [who was in the house] [and who knew Mary] left" is OK (two
relative clauses). NOT OK: "The army came to Jerusalem [and defeated the
Jews]" (that's a separate independent clause) — write "The army came to
Jerusalem. And the army defeated the Jews." (No brackets for He1 either way.)

0.23. English "of" sometimes means "named" — don't write "nation of Israel",
write "nation named Israel" (and similarly elsewhere).

0.24. Don't use "can" — normally becomes "is able [to ..." (no brackets for
He1).

0.25. Watch "to": not at the start of an event/adverbial subordinate clause
(though it can start a patient clause like "John wants [to meet Mary]" when
the subordinate agent = main-clause agent). Often people mean
"in-order-to". There's no generic "to" relation — only use "to" in a PP if
the verb takes a destination argument. (No brackets for He1.)

0.26. Don't inflect hyphenated words: "John stand-up", not "John stood-up".

0.27. Verbs take only one direct object (the noun argument not preceded by
"to"/"with"): "John gave a gift to Mary", not "John gave Mary a gift".

0.28. Don't use "going to" for future tense — use "will": "John will see
Mary", not "John is going to see Mary".

0.29. No double negatives: "All people love their mothers.", not "No person
does not love his mother."

0.30. When a complex word is represented via pairing/explication, the
sentence must make sense with either the simple or the complex reading — the
available target-language vocabulary is unknown in advance.

0.31. No participles/gerunds ("Teaching is fun") — reword: "[A person
teaches people _implicit] is-V fun" (He1: "It is fun that a person teaches
<<people>>." — no brackets for He1).

0.32. "only" as adverb (new: "only-A", for exclusivity) is now allowed:
"John only prayed in the temple." The adjective "only" still has 3 senses —
identity, exclusivity, low esteem, number-modification — and low-esteem
"only" is not the same as the new adverb: not "...only helped a few people"
(low esteem) but "...helped only-B a few people" (adjective, low esteem
sense).

0.33. Avoid negative polarity ("not") on a verb combined with causal clauses
("because"/"so-A"/"so-C") — the scope is ambiguous in our system (word order
doesn't disambiguate it the way it does in English). Two different real
meanings for "The office did not hire Jane because Jane is the boss's
daughter" illustrate the problem. Acceptable exceptions: (a) when context
makes the meaning clear regardless, or (b) when avoiding the construction
would be too awkward otherwise — but avoid true ambiguity where you can.

0.34. Avoid vague back-references like "did that action//thing" ("//" marks
an either-word option) — be concrete about what happened, except where
that's genuinely necessary.

0.36. Write a given noun the same way everywhere in a sentence: "Paul gave
letters to those men [so that those men would read LETTERS]", not "...read
THOSE LETTERS" (2nd+ mentions may be assumed to follow the first). Less
strict for He1 — pronouns are sometimes fine there; no brackets for He1.

0.37. Don't use a passive that couldn't be converted to active. "John went to
Bethlehem [in order to be seen by the Pharisees]" is invalid because its
active form ("...in order to the Pharisees see John") breaks the
in-order-to same-agent requirement. (The semantic agent is "Pharisees" even
in the passive surface form.)

0.38. "in prison//court" is English-specific — use "in a//the//that
prison//court".

0.39. English "when" often really means "after" — if the main clause's
action clearly follows the adverbial clause's action, use "after": "[After
John saw Jesus] John was happy."

0.40. Apposition (a second noun restating the first, e.g. "Yahweh, your
God") is not allowed except for addressees: "Lord, my(David's) God, you(God)
are great" is OK, but "The Lord, my(David's) God, is great" (non-addressee
agent) is not. Distinct from a noun-noun relation like "army hat" = "hat of
army" (generic genitive).

0.41. Use "which X" for an interrogative argument: "What" → "which thing-A",
but prefer "which thing" plus the intended sense of "thing" unless
"thing-A" is really meant. "Which" can now also appear in non-questions:
"John did not know [which house Mary was living in]" (or without brackets in
He1).

0.42. Commas: needed to separate an addressee from the rest of a sentence,
after "X said" + direct quote, and in coordinate noun phrases ("apples,
oranges, and berries" — Oxford comma). Optional (no effect) elsewhere.

0.43. Avoid "let"/"allow" with a subordinate clause whose agent is
"God//Yahweh//Lord", even where a natural-sounding English passive exists —
not all target languages have a passive, and converting to active would
wrongly cast something/someone as "letting/allowing" God act. Avoid "You
(imp) do not allow [the people to be destroyed by Yahweh
_implicitActiveAgent]." ("Do not allow the people to be destroyed <<by
Yahweh>>." in He1) — its active form would wrongly read "Do not allow Yahweh
to destroy the people."

0.44. Use only the senses our system's words actually have — don't stretch a
word to a sense we don't support.

0.45. No "make" sense of causing an adjectival characteristic — not "X made Y
good"; use "X caused [Y to be good]" ("X caused Y to be good" in He1).

0.47. "Both A and B" (both + two separate nouns) isn't currently supported —
"both" only goes with a dual noun ("both men _dual").

0.48. Only a verb with a patient noun can be passivized; the patient becomes
the agent of the passive sentence.

0.49. Use "obey" or "obey/submit" for obeying laws/instructions, not
"follow-B" (reserved for following people).

0.50. Use "give" for offering sacrifices, not "offer" (which means asking
someone if they want something).

0.51. Imperatives with epistemic verbs (know, understand, realize, ...) or
emotion verbs (e.g. "You(people) (imp) be happy" / "Be happy" in He1) are now
allowed (previously discouraged).

0.52. Nouns that take a noun argument ("faith in X", "relationship with X")
should generally use that construction rather than a relative clause.

0.53. Poetry: mark indented poetry sections with "(begin-poetry)" /
"(end-poetry)"; mark blank-line separations within poetry (as in the NIV)
with "(blank-line)". Doesn't apply to non-poetry block quotations — just use
quote marks for those. "(title)" is always followed by "(paragraph)" even if
the NIV doesn't show a new paragraph there for poetry. "(poetry-begin)" /
"(poetry-end)" are internal to a sentence/clause and must be repeated across
alternate sentences; "(paragraph)" / "(blank-line)" are external to
sentences and are not repeated.

0.54. For "a SHORT time" / "a LONG time" where units can't be used, write
"for a time-short-<units>" / "for a time-long-<units>" (units = a time unit
like minutes/years/days) — e.g. "for a time-long-years" may generate as "for
many years" downstream.
